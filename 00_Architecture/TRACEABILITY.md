## 1. Introduction
This document outlines the traceability framework within the AITOS v2 system, detailing how every decision, from raw market data ingestion to final trade execution, can be fully explained and audited. Traceability is a cornerstone of AITOS, ensuring transparency, accountability, and the ability to debug and improve the system effectively.

## 2. Purpose
The primary purpose of this traceability model is to provide a clear, step-by-step explanation for any system output or decision. This is crucial for:
*   **Debugging and Optimization:** Quickly identifying the root cause of unexpected behavior or suboptimal performance.
*   **Regulatory Compliance:** Meeting requirements for auditability and transparency in financial markets.
*   **Trust and Confidence:** Building confidence in the system's operations among stakeholders.
*   **Continuous Learning:** Facilitating the analysis of past decisions to inform future improvements.

## 3. Core Principle: Full Explainability
Every decision made by the AITOS system, at any layer or module, must be fully explainable. This means that for any given output, it must be possible to reconstruct the exact sequence of inputs, algorithms, and intermediate states that led to that output.

## 4. Traceability Mechanism
Traceability in AITOS is achieved through a combination of:
*   **Unique Identifiers:** Every event, signal, decision, and transaction is assigned a unique ID.
*   **Logging:** Comprehensive, timestamped logs capture all significant events, module inputs/outputs, and internal states.
*   **Metadata Propagation:** Key metadata (e.g., source module, version, confidence scores) is propagated through the data pipeline.
*   **Interface Contracts:** Strict interface contracts (`06_Interfaces`) ensure predictable data structures and transformations.
*   **Knowledge Graphs:** (Future) A centralized knowledge graph can link related entities and decisions.

## 5. Example Data Flow Trace
Consider a scenario where a trade is executed. The traceability framework allows us to trace back the decision-making process:

```mermaid
graph TD
    Signal[Signal (e.g., from AITOS-ALPHA)] --> MarketStructure[Market Structure (AITOS-MSTRUCT-11)]
    MarketStructure --> Liquidity[Liquidity (from AITOS-MICRO-08)]
    Liquidity --> OrderBlock[Order Block (from AITOS-SMC)]
    OrderBlock --> FVG[Fair Value Gap (from AITOS-SMC)]
    FVG --> RiskEngine[Risk Engine (AITOS-RISK)]
    RiskEngine --> AILayer[AI Layer (AITOS-AI)]
    AILayer --> Execution[Execution (AITOS-EXEC)]
```

**Explanation of Trace:**
*   **Execution:** The trade was executed by `AITOS-EXEC` based on instructions from `AITOS-AI`.
*   **AI Layer:** `AITOS-AI` provided an override or final approval based on the risk assessment from `AITOS-RISK`.
*   **Risk Engine:** `AITOS-RISK` evaluated the trade's risk profile, considering factors derived from `FVG`, `Order Block`, and `Liquidity`.
*   **FVG & Order Block:** These concepts, identified by `AITOS-SMC`, indicated specific market conditions that influenced the signal.
*   **Liquidity:** Information about available liquidity from `AITOS-MICRO-08` informed the potential impact and feasibility of the trade.
*   **Market Structure:** The prevailing market structure, identified by `AITOS-MSTRUCT-11`, provided context for interpreting the signal.
*   **Signal:** The initial trading signal was generated by `AITOS-ALPHA` based on various analytical inputs.

Every step in this chain is linked by unique identifiers and logged events, allowing for a complete reconstruction of the decision.

## 6. Conclusion
The AITOS traceability framework ensures that the system's operations are transparent and auditable. By meticulously linking every decision to its underlying data and logic, we empower developers, researchers, and regulators to fully understand and verify the system's behavior.
